# License of a parcel brand versus a separate white-label app (2025–2026)

Research date: 25 October 2026. Figures below are copied from the cited page in the currency that page uses. No exchange rates were applied. Where a source itself prints a USD figure, that figure is quoted as the source’s. Nothing here is a recommended price for Deliservy.

Two commercial directions are easy to mix up, and the published numbers mostly cover the first:

- **Operator’s brand on the vendor’s software.** The operator pays a setup and/or a monthly or per-task fee. The vendor keeps the code. The customer-facing name becomes the operator’s. This is what Tookan, Onro Enterprise, Shipday Branded Elite, Wodely White-Label, and iCargos sell.
- **Platform’s brand, app, and demand; a local company runs the work.** The public parcel examples are shipping-counter franchises (Mexico) and Amazon’s Delivery Service Partner program. In the franchises, the operator pays an entry fee and sometimes a royalty, and the franchisor keeps the mark and the system. In Amazon DSP, the money runs the other way: Amazon pays the operator, and Amazon keeps the mark, the app, and the packages.

No public 2024–2026 price was found for the exact structure “one exclusive city licensee operates inside the owner’s existing consumer app and parcel panel, pays a royalty, and may not rebrand.”

## What do white-label courier-app vendors publish as setup plus monthly?

### Takeaway
Published courier-software prices are monthly or annual SaaS, sometimes plus a one-time setup, almost always for the operator’s own brand on the vendor’s hosted system. A full separate white-label mobile app is either a higher annual bundle (Tookan), a custom enterprise tier with an unpublished or “minimal” setup fee (Onro), or a five-figure setup plus a much higher monthly minimum (Wodely). Operating inside the vendor’s own consumer brand is not what these price pages sell.

### Inferences
- The cheap public number is a seat in the vendor’s hosted dispatcher (tens to a few hundred USD a month, or a few euros on iCargos). The expensive public number is a separately listed white-label app: Tookan’s one-year bundle from $5,999, Wodely’s $9,000 plus $800/month, or Onro Enterprise with an unpublished setup.
- “1 year validity” on Tookan’s “one time cost” means the white-label bundle expires and is not a sale of the software.
- Shipday’s branded tier is still Shipday’s system with the operator’s logo. It is not a license to operate under the Shipday consumer brand.

## What do small Latin American parcel franchises publish as entry fee, royalty, and retained ownership?

### Takeaway
The public Mexican numbers are for shipping counters that resell DHL, FedEx, Estafeta and similar carriers. They are not fees to run an exclusive city fleet inside the franchisor’s consumer app. Entry fees cluster in the low hundreds of thousands of pesos, royalties are either a fixed monthly peso amount or a percent of sales, and the franchisor keeps the mark, the counter system, and the carrier contracts. Venezuela’s MRW network does not publish a fee.

### Cited Findings

**EnvíoShop (brand site, fetched 25 Oct 2026).** The company describes itself as a parcel counter that concentrates DHL, FedEx, UPS, Estafeta, Paquetexpress, Redpack, and J&T. The page says the franchisee does not deliver in the street; the franchisor supplies the technology. It offers a “zona exclusiva,” 12 initial months without royalties, a royalty that is “fija y congelada” (no peso amount on this page), and contract renewal at no cost. Figures printed: $169,000 MXN + IVA, $130,000 MXN + IVA, and $299,000 MXN + IVA, plus a US package of $27,000, $23,000 + tax, and about $50,000 + tax. The three MXN lines are not labeled in the extracted text; the 2025 PDF (below) labels $169,000 as the franchise fee. — [EnvíoShop franquicias](https://www.envioshop.mx/franquicias)

**EnvíoShop 2025 corporate PDF.** Franchise fee $169,000 + IVA; approximate fit-out $145,000 + IVA; approximate total $314,000 + IVA; 12 months without royalties; contract term 5 years; corporate ad fund included in the monthly royalty. The PDF’s fit-out and total differ from the live site’s $130,000 and $299,000 lines. The royalty’s peso amount is not readable in the extracted PDF text. — [EnvíoShop 2025 presentation](https://www.envioshop.mx/_files/ugd/87b1d7_13a700a9c4904b429c6c29129d158b6c.pdf)

**Franquicias al Día (directory, not the franchisor).** EnvíoShop: entry canon MXN $169,000 + IVA; total investment MXN $299,000 + IVA; royalty $2,900 + IVA from month 13; ad canon included in the royalty; contract 5 years, renewable. — [Franquicias al Día, EnvíoShop](https://www.franquiciasaldia.com.mx/franquicias/franquicias-de-servicios-postales/Franquicia-envioshop/)

**Expansión, 8 April 2026 (business press, citing the Asociación Mexicana de Franquicias directory).** It reports a CEP market of $6,600 million in Mexico in 2025 (IMARC) and lists:

- EnvioExpress: franchise fee 342,400 pesos including VAT, which the article also breaks as 242,400 initial fee plus 100,000 fit-out; total investment the same 342,400 including VAT; royalty 2,500 pesos + VAT from month eight, first six start-up months forgiven; ad fund included in the royalty.
- Empakatodo: franchise fee 80,000 pesos + VAT; total investment 400,000 pesos + VAT; no royalty.
- A $169,000 + VAT fee and $2,900 + VAT royalty from month 13 (the EnvíoShop block; the article’s total investment here is 359,000 pesos, which matches neither the brand site’s $299,000 nor the 2025 PDF’s $314,000).
- Mail Boxes Etc.: franchise fee 490,000 pesos; total investment 950,000–1,200,000 pesos; royalty 6%; media fund 3%.

The article says AMF’s estimated range for parcel franchises is 250,000 to 700,000 pesos. — [Expansión, 8 Apr 2026](https://expansion.mx/empresas/2026/04/08/paqueteria-es-negocio-6-600-mdd-mexico-abrir-franquicia)

**TuFranquicia.com.mx (directory).** Mail Boxes Etc.: investment from $800,000 MXN; brand fee $490,000 MXN; royalties 6%; ad fund “2% de Publicidad Institucional + 1% de Publicidad”; contract 10 years; 62 franchised units. The directory tells the reader to confirm figures with the brand. — [TuFranquicia, Mail Boxes Etc.](https://tufranquicia.com.mx/franquicias/mail-boxes-etc)

**Mail Boxes Etc. México official franchise page.** Describes a proven system, technology tools, and authorized carrier alliances. It does not print the fee, the royalty, or the percent. It states that MBE is an authorized intermediary and that FedEx, DHL, and the other marks belong to their owners. — [MBE venta de franquicias](https://www.mbemx.com/ventafranquicias)

**Paquete Llegue Express (brand site).** Total investment $350,000.00 net, franchise fee included. Includes use of the brand for 5 years, renewable; access to agreements with DHL, UPS, FedEx, Estafeta, AMPM, Tresguerras, Redpack, Sendex, and J&T; initial training and ongoing technical assistance. No royalty is stated. The prospect is offered the franchise-offer circular after applying. — [Paquete Llegue Express franquicias](https://paquetelleguexpress.com/franquicias/)

**MRW Spain (franchise directory, not MRW’s own tariff).** Entry fee: none. Royalty: a percent per sales unit (percent not printed). Advertising canon: a percent per sales unit (not printed). Exclusive zone: yes. Contract: 2 years. Minimum premises 30 m2. — [Mundofranquicia, MRW](https://www.mundofranquicia.com/franquicia/transporte-mensajeria/mrw/)

**MRW Venezuela.** The authorized-agent page invites operators and lists training and advice. It prints no entry fee and no royalty. — [MRW Venezuela, agentes autorizados](https://mrwve.com/agentes-autorizados)

**Older trade press, not a current fee.** A 2010-era report of MRW’s then general manager says Venezuela franchisees prepaid for closed-price shipping vouchers (“vales”), so both the franchisee and MRW collected before the shipment, because credit risk was high. Spain, by contrast, billed the franchisee and gave 30-day credit. No peso or bolívar fee is in that account. — [Negocius](https://www.negocius.com.mx/mrw-encuentra-las-claves-para-invertir-en-venezuela_wv2097.html)

### Inferences
- Where a number is public, the franchisor is selling a counter, a mark, a cloud POS or rate tool, and wholesale carrier contracts. The operator’s margin is the spread on those carriers, not a fleet’s delivery margin.
- The only percent-of-sales royalty in this set is Mail Boxes Etc. at 6% plus about 3% advertising, and that percent is in a 2026 news article and a franchise directory, not on MBE’s own franchise page.
- EnvíoShop’s stable public entry fee is $169,000 MXN + IVA. The all-in investment is not stable across the 2025 PDF ($314,000), the live site ($299,000), and Expansión ($359,000). The $2,900 monthly royalty is directory and trade-press, not on the page fetched from the brand.

### Gaps
- No public circular de oferta de franquicia (the Mexican disclosure document) was retrieved; Paquete Llegue says it sends one after an application.
- No public fee for Zoom, Domesa, Tealca, or MRW Venezuela.
- No public Latin American fee for an exclusive messenger fleet (motos) under a local brand, as opposed to a counter.
- Servientrega / Interrapidísimo agency fees were not found with a citable number in this pass.

## What do logistics SaaS partner programs publish when a local company “operates a city”?

### Takeaway
Onfleet and Bringg publish partner programs with commissions or ambassador referrals. Neither publishes a revenue-share percent, a city fee, or regional exclusivity for an operator of the vendor’s brand. The closest sourced “local company runs the city under our brand and our app” contract is Amazon DSP, and there Amazon pays the operator.

### Cited Findings

**Onfleet partner page.** Affiliate partners “earn one-time commissions on successful referrals.” Reseller partners “earn one-time commissions or recurring revenue,” may sell a bundled solution, and may embed Onfleet. A marketplace track lists Onfleet carriers and couriers and sends the marketplace shipper referrals. No percent, no city fee, and no exclusivity. — [Onfleet partnerships](https://onfleet.com/partnerships). A  press release on the Drive program repeats the same structure (tiered reseller, referral link, automatic payment) and still prints no rate. — [PRWeb, Onfleet partnership ecosystem](https://www.prweb.com/releases/onfleet-welcomes-cory-woolf-as-chief-financial-officer-and-announces-new-partnership-ecosystem-875039074.html)

**Onfleet customer referral, not a reseller rate (support article updated 25 Oct 2024).** Bronze $300, Silver $500, Gold $750 cash per successful referral, only if the referred customer stays at least 3 months. Paid through PartnerStack. — [Onfleet referral program](https://support.onfleet.com/hc/en-us/articles/30850928366612-Onfleet-s-Referral-Program)

**Bringg.** The alliances lead, in an interview, describes technological integrations and an ambassador program of consultants who help Bringg’s sales team. No commission percent. — [Group 14, Bringg Q&A](https://group14.substack.com/p/bringg-follow-up-qa). Bringg’s terms limit the software license to “the territory set forth in the Service Order.” That is a contractual territory field, not a published exclusive-city price. — [Bringg terms](https://www.bringg.com/tos)

**Amazon DSP, direction of payment (current program materials).** US program page: startup “with as little as $10,000” by using deals Amazon negotiated. — [Amazon Vendor Exchange](https://vendorexchange.amazon/). The English DSP brochure defines that $10,000 as an estimate of essential startup expenses for the first five vehicles, assuming the operator uses Amazon-negotiated vehicle, insurance, device, data, and uniform deals. It is not described there as a fee paid to Amazon for the brand. The same brochure says fully ramped revenue potential of $1M–$4.5M and profit potential of $75K–$300K, and says those revenue and profit figures rest on 2021 results volunteered by a subset of standard-delivery companies. Payment structure in the brochure: a fixed monthly amount based on the number of vans operated with Amazon, a route rate based on route length, and a per-package rate for successfully delivered packages. — [Amazon DSP brochure PDF](https://m.media-amazon.com/images/G/01/DSP2022/assets/desktop/DSP_Brochure_English_V4.pdf); payment bullets also in [DSP brochure, startup and revenue](https://m.media-amazon.com/images/G/01/DSP2/DSP_Brochure_English_Final.pdf). UK 2026 brochure: startup as low as £10,000; annual profit potential £50K–£150K; same style of estimate and the same warning that results are not guaranteed. — [Amazon DSP UK 2026 brochure](https://m.media-amazon.com/images/G/02/DSP2022/assets/desktop/DSP_2026_UK_Brochure.pdf). Amazon’s letter to Sen. Murphy (the PDF) repeats that startup costs, including vans, were designed to be as low as $10,000, and that rates paid to DSPs are calculated to cover insuring, maintaining, and operating the fleet. — [Amazon response to DSP inquiry](https://www.murphy.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/amazon_response_to_sen_murphy_dsp_inquiry_feb_2023.pdf)

A secondary 2026 blog treats the $10,000 as an “application fee paid directly to Amazon” and adds a $30,000 liquid-asset figure. The Amazon brochure does not describe a $10,000 application fee. Do not use the blog’s fee characterization. — [Ad Badger](https://www.adbadger.com/blog/how-much-does-it-cost-to-start-an-amazon-dsp/)

### Inferences
- A published “recurring revenue” reseller program (Onfleet) is a software commission for introducing fleet customers. It is not a license to own a city under Onfleet’s consumer brand.
- Amazon DSP is the structural cousin of “you use our app and our mark, you hire the couriers, you keep an operating margin,” except the platform is the customer and the payer. There is no published royalty from the operator to Amazon.

### Gaps
- Onfleet commission percent and whether any reseller gets territorial exclusivity.
- Bringg ambassador or reseller percent.
- Any “powered by” logistics program that publishes a percent of the local operator’s delivery revenue.
- FedEx ISP / UPS contractor fee schedules were not pulled in this pass.

## Which contract limits are actually written down?

### Takeaway
The sourced software terms keep the code, ban reverse engineering and resale, and end access when the vendor suspends or the term ends. Customer-uploaded data is often the customer’s, with a short export window. White-label apps, where mentioned, stay the vendor’s property even when they carry the customer’s logo. Territory exclusivity for a single operator of the vendor’s brand is not in these SaaS terms. Amazon’s DSP license is nonexclusive, revocable, and limited to performing Amazon’s deliveries.

### Cited Findings

**Tookan terms (vendor terms page).** Tookan may terminate, suspend, or modify the service, generally or for a given customer, without cause or prior notice, and may delete content or data at its discretion. The customer may not reverse engineer, decompile, or extract source code, and may not rent, lease, or sublicense access. The license is “limited, non-exclusive, non-transferable,” for internal use “and not for resale or further distribution.” Software, improvements, customizations, and “white-label branded applications” “are and shall remain the sole and exclusive property of Tookan.” If Tookan makes a white-label app with the customer’s branding, the customer grants Tookan a perpetual, worldwide, royalty-free license to use the customer’s trademarks on that app. If an amount is 30 or more days overdue (10 days if it was a card charge), Tookan may accelerate unpaid fees and suspend the service until paid, after at least 6 days’ notice that the account is overdue. The customer may not assign or sublicense without Tookan’s written consent. User data is collected and stored to provide the service; Tookan may use aggregated data; the customer is given access to user data and the ability to delete it. The terms do not say the customer owns that data. — [Tookan terms of service](https://jungleworks.com/tookan/terms-of-service/)

**Onfleet terms.** “All data uploaded by Customer remains the property of Customer,” excluding Operational Data. Operational Data (route and GPS information, device identifiers, diagnostic logs, performance data, aggregated analytics) is owned by Onfleet. Onfleet gets a royalty-free license to process customer data only to perform the agreement. The software, workflows, and interface “remain only with Onfleet.” The customer may not remove proprietary markings. Within 60 days after termination, on request, Onfleet makes the service available so the customer can export customer data; after that, Onfleet may destroy it and has no duty to keep it. Trial data may be deleted if the account does not convert. Mutual termination for material breach follows a 30-day notice and cure period. The API license is non-exclusive, nontransferable, and terminable. The fetched terms do not grant source code and do not grant an exclusive territory. — [Onfleet terms](https://onfleet.com/terms)

**Bringg terms.** License is personal, non-exclusive, and non-transferable, and “may be used only in the territory set forth in the Service Order.” No other interest in the software passes. The customer may not reverse engineer or extract source code. All improvements and “white-label branded applications” remain Bringg’s property. As between the parties, the customer owns personally identifiable information of its users, end customers, and mobile workforce. Fixed fees are invoiced annually in advance, are non-refundable, and the order is non-cancelable. If payment is late, Bringg may suspend the services and/or terminate for breach. Initial term is as in the order; then automatic one-year renewals unless either party gives at least 90 days’ notice. If the agreement ends for any reason other than Bringg’s uncured material breach, the customer owes the fees for the rest of the then-current term. On any termination the license ends and Bringg stops providing the services. The customer may not assign or sublicense without consent. — [Bringg terms](https://www.bringg.com/tos)

**Onro pricing-page answers, not a full terms document.** Business has no setup fee. Enterprise has a minimal setup fee. “There’s no long-term contract. You just pay as you go.” Stopping payment is described as simply not using the product, with no penalty. Custom domains and removal of Onro branding are Enterprise-only. — [Onro pricing](https://onro.io/pricing/)

**Shipday terms (older host, smilodon-ellipsoid-z5fa.squarespace.com/terms).** The company may suspend or terminate access for any reason, with or without notice, and without liability. The user may terminate by stopping use. The extracted clauses do not assign ownership of customer data and do not mention white-label or territory. — [Shipday terms on Squarespace host](https://smilodon-ellipsoid-z5fa.squarespace.com/terms). A page at shipday.one is a different product (PIMMS) and is not used here.

**Relay marketing FAQ, not a contract.** Relay’s white-label page says the customer owns brand, customer relationships, and order data and can take a full copy, while Relay owns the engine. Exit terms are “written into the agreement before launch.” If Relay publishes the apps, “our developer name may appear on the app-store listing.” This is the vendor’s sales page. — [Relay white-label](https://relayapp.ng/white-label)

**Amazon Logistics DSP Program Agreement, exhibit labeled “Last Updated: October 2021” (older than the 2024–2026 preference; still the public contract text found).** During the term Amazon grants a limited, nonexclusive, non-transferable, non-sublicensable, revocable license to use tools, software, applications, technology, content, and trademarks solely to perform the services. Personnel may not copy, distribute, sublicense, modify, decompile, reverse engineer, or make derivative works. Amazon pays the company under program policies (monthly per-vehicle component, and the policies can stop that component for excess vehicles on 30 days’ notice if routes are reduced). The company may not use Amazon trademarks except as the agreement permits. On termination or non-renewal the company must help transition the services to Amazon or another provider Amazon names, and Amazon is not liable for the company’s lost investment. A company may choose “branded” or “unbranded”; branded status has extra requirements, and seasonal workers may wear an Amazon vest instead of full Amazon clothing. The license clause does not grant an exclusive territory. — [CourtListener, Exhibit B, Amazon Logistics DSP agreement](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.wawd.308790/gov.uscourts.wawd.308790.33.2.pdf). A Canada version on Law Insider follows the same license structure; many time periods are redacted. — [Law Insider, Amazon Canada DSP agreement](https://www.lawinsider.com/contracts/8KJvr4Krfk)

**Franchise brand limits that are on the franchisor’s own page.** Paquete Llegue Express: the $350,000 buys use of the brand for five years, renewable, plus carrier agreements and training. — [Paquete Llegue Express](https://paquetelleguexpress.com/franquicias/). EnvíoShop’s live page sells an exclusive zone and says the franchisor provides the technology; it does not grant source code. — [EnvíoShop](https://www.envioshop.mx/franquicias)

### Inferences
- Across Tookan, Onfleet, and Bringg, “white label” does not mean the operator owns the app. The branded binary stays with the vendor, and the operator’s logo is a trademark license the other way (operator licenses its mark to the vendor so the vendor can paint the app).
- Nonpayment consequences that are actually written: Tookan may suspend after notice once the invoice is 10 or 30 days overdue; Bringg may suspend or terminate if payment is late; Onfleet’s fetched terms use a 30-day cure for material breach and a 60-day export window, and do not spell out a card-decline rule in the sections reviewed.
- Exclusive territory shows up in franchise marketing (EnvíoShop; MRW’s directory entry), not in the courier-SaaS terms. Amazon’s DSP license is expressly nonexclusive.

### Gaps
- Shipday’s current shipday.com terms were not found as a full ownership clause; the Squarespace terms above may be stale.
- Onro has no fetched terms page, only pricing-page answers.
- No Mexican COF was read, so franchise rules on customer-list ownership and post-termination de-identification are not sourced.
- The Amazon agreement’s data-ownership section was not extracted clause by clause; only license, payment direction, trademark control, and transition-on-exit were.

## What published price gap exists between “use our brand and system” and “we build you a separate white-label app”?

### Takeaway
No single vendor publishes both a fee to operate inside that vendor’s consumer brand and a fee to receive a separate white-label app. The gaps that are printed are (1) same-vendor SaaS versus a white-label app that carries the buyer’s brand, and (2) a different market, Mexican counter franchises, where the operator pays to use the franchisor’s brand. Those two gaps should not be subtracted from each other.

### Inferences
- On the only like-for-like pages, putting the buyer’s brand on a separate app costs more than using the vendor’s dispatcher: Wodely’s white-label floor is $9,000 plus $800/month against a $49 or $159 dispatcher; Tookan’s white-label year starts at $5,999 against a monthly plan that can be $99. Shipday’s step from “their tracking” to “your logo on the driver app” is $50/month at the list price ($49 to $99) and a higher per-order overage ($0.10 to $0.20), still on one codebase.
- Paying to wear the platform’s brand is a franchise or a DSP relationship. In the Mexican counter sample the operator pays the brand owner. In Amazon DSP the brand owner pays the operator. Neither sample is a published percent-of-revenue city license for a parcel app the owner refuses to rebrand.

### Gaps
- A vendor that prints both numbers for the same parcel product: fee to run city X under the vendor’s name, and fee to receive a de-branded copy of the same app.
- Any revenue-share percent tied to that city license.
