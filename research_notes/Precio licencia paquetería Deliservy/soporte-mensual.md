# Monthly support retainers for a small parcel platform

Research date: 25 October 2026. Scope: what a small operator pays for human support of one extra tenant of an existing parcel panel and app (how to use it, incidents, configuration, training on an existing operating model). Not a new product build and not on-site operations management. No price is recommended here. The founder’s guesses ($200/month to operate the system; $300–$800/month for support) are mapped onto published figures only.

## What do Latin American agencies and freelance engineers charge per hour and per monthly retainer in 2025–2026?

### Takeaway
Published 2026 bands put Latin American freelance developers around $35–$75/hour as a regional average, Colombian marketplace contracts around $26/hour mid-level and $34/hour senior (developer rate, not a client invoice), and Colombian agencies on Clutch mostly in the $25–$49 or $50–$99/hour bands. Full-time LatAm engineers sold through marketplaces cost roughly $4,600–$10,700 per month all-in by seniority, which is a different product from a support retainer. No source found breaks out Venezuela hourly rates.

### Cited Findings
- Arc’s own pricing page lists freelance hires at **$15–$110+ per hour**, paid for hours worked, with no separate Arc fee on the freelance rate. Full-time placement is listed as **20% of annual salary**. The page does not split that hourly range by seniority or country. — [Arc pricing](https://arc.dev/pricing)
- Arc’s Colombia and Mexico hiring pages, both headed October 2026, repeat the same freelance range: rates **from $15 to $110+**, set by the freelancer, no extra fees. They do not publish a Colombia-only or Venezuela band. — [Arc Colombia](https://arc.dev/en-co/hire-developers); [Arc Mexico](https://arc.dev/en-mx/hire-developers)
- Arc’s 2026 employer guide says it surveyed **5,302 freelance developers worldwide** and then presents regional ranges that it explicitly describes as “broad market observations across freelance marketplaces, recruiter benchmarks, startup hiring data, and Arc’s historical survey data rather than fixed salary bands.” Latin America is **$35–$75/hour**. The same page’s global experience table (not a LatAm table) is junior (1–3 years) **$20–$50/hour**, mid-level (3–6 years) **$40–$90/hour**, senior (7–10 years) **$75–$150/hour**, staff/principal (10+ years) **$120–$220/hour**. City rows labeled “average senior freelance rate” include Medellín **$35–$65/hour** and Buenos Aires **$38–$75/hour**. Venezuela is not listed. — [Arc freelance developer rates 2026](https://arc.dev/employer-blog/freelance-developers-cost/)
- A competitor’s October 2026 write-up says Arc’s FAQ states that most Arc developers charge **$60–$100+/hour**. That sentence was not on the pricing-page text retrieved for this note, so it is second-hand. The same write-up converts Arc’s $15–$110+ band to about **$2,400–$17,600+ per month at 160 hours**, and says that 160-hour month is the author’s math, not Arc’s quote. — [Ryz Labs on Arc pricing](https://www.ryzlabs.com/vs/arc-pricing)
- Revelo’s software-engineer hiring page publishes all-in monthly cost (engineer pay, PEO coverage, and Revelo’s margin): junior **$4,600–$5,600**, mid-level **$5,800–$7,500**, senior **$7,200–$10,700**. Month-to-month, no placement fee. It does not publish an hourly rate or a country split. — [Revelo software engineers](https://www.revelo.com/hire/software-engineers)
- Revelo’s pricing page, for a senior full-stack “elite” comparison, shows a LatAm all-in figure of **$134,000 per year** against a US figure of **$327,986 per year**. A FAQ answer on the same page says a senior full-stack with 6+ years “commands **$111k–$128k** through Revelo.” Those annual figures sit at or above the top of the $7,200–$10,700 monthly band on the other Revelo page ($10,700 × 12 = $128,400). Revelo does not explain the gap between “elite” calculator output and the broader seniority band. — [Revelo pricing](https://www.revelo.com/pricing)
- Terminal’s Latin America contractor page gives median contractor rates of **$9,789/month (Chile) to $10,642/month (Costa Rica)**, and says rates are relatively comparable across Chile, Colombia, Costa Rica, and Mexico. It says Colombia has the region’s second-highest contractor rates but does not print a Colombia dollar median on the retrieved text. It also says DevOps is the highest-paid LatAm contractor role, and names data engineer at **$10,598/month** as a high-paid role in one heading and DevOps in the body. The page does not date the dataset in the retrieved text. — [Terminal LatAm contractor rates](https://www.terminal.io/remote-software-engineer-salaries/latin-america-contractor)
- A 2026 third-party summary repeats Terminal’s **$9,789–$10,642/month** contractor band and full-time medians of **$103,075–$106,918 per year**, and says Terminal’s fee can sit on top of the engineer’s pay. Treat the fee caveat as that author’s reading. Separately, Terminal’s own 2026 marketing says engineering hires “usually” cost **$4,000–$12,000**, plus a small onboarding fee. That wider band is not the same statistic as the country medians. — [South on Terminal pricing](https://www.hireinsouth.com/post/terminal-pricing); [Terminal on Globant competitors](https://www.terminal.io/blog/5-globant-competitors-for-software-development-in-2025)
- Clutch’s software-pricing guide, retrieved as an October 2026 page, lists Mexico custom-software companies at an average of **$25–$49/hour**. It does not list Colombia or Venezuela in the country table that was retrieved. United States on the same table is **$50–$99/hour**. — [Clutch developer pricing](https://clutch.co/developers/pricing)
- Clutch’s Colombia developer directory, with “Ratings Updated: Oct 24, 2026,” shows named agencies in two hourly bands and does not split junior, mid, and senior. Examples: Teravision Technologies (Bogotá, 250–999 employees) **$50–$99/hour**, minimum project **$25,000+**; HatchWorks AI (Medellín, 250–999) **$50–$99/hour**, minimum **$25,000+**; Activeone (Medellín, 50–249) **$25–$49/hour**, minimum **$10,000+**; Wawandco (Barranquilla, 10–49) **$25–$49/hour**, minimum **$25,000+**; CodeBranch (Medellín, 10–49) **$25–$49/hour**, minimum **$25,000+**. Leanware (Bogotá, 10–49) is listed at **$50–$99/hour** with a **$50,000+** minimum. These are agency project rates, not published monthly support retainers. — [Clutch Colombia developers](https://clutch.co/co/developers?page=6); [Clutch Colombia AI developers](https://clutch.co/co/developers/artificial-intelligence)
- Lemon.io’s Colombia page, “last updated: Q3 2026,” says the figures are developer rates on active contracts in its network, “not a self-reported survey, not a client price,” and that juniors (0–2 years) are excluded. Across **606 contracts**: mid-level median **$26/hour**, senior median **$34/hour**, strong-senior **$43/hour**. It says the network-wide senior median is **$45/hour**, so Colombia is 24% below that network median. Annualizing assumes **2,080 hours**, which is Lemon’s assumption. — [Lemon.io Colombia rates](https://lemon.io/rate-calculator/colombia/)
- An outsourcing vendor’s 2026 country guide attributes to Clutch a Latin America agency range of **$25–$199/hour** (Mexico, Colombia, Brazil, Argentina) and Colombia specifically **$25–$199/hour**, Brazil and Argentina and Mexico **$25–$149/hour**. That page is an aggregator, not Clutch itself, and the range is much wider than the named Colombian profiles above. The same page predicts mid-level and senior rates will rise **3–6% in 2026**; that sentence is the vendor’s forecast, not a measured result. — [Qubit Labs offshore rates](https://qubit-labs.com/average-hourly-rates-offshore-development-services-software-development-costs-guide/)

### Inferences
- Seniority splits that are actually published are Revelo’s monthly all-in bands (LatAm, full-time) and Arc’s global freelance table. Arc’s LatAm **$35–$75** band is a regional blend, not a junior/mid/senior card. Lemon’s Colombia medians are the only 2026 seniority split tied to Colombia, and they are the developer’s rate, so a client paying an agency would be higher.
- A support retainer is not what these marketplace pages sell. Revelo senior at **$7,200–$10,700/month** and Terminal medians near **$10,000/month** are a person for a month. The founder’s **$300–$800** support guess is about 3% to 11% of the bottom and top of Revelo’s senior band ($300 / $10,700 ≈ 2.8%; $800 / $7,200 ≈ 11%). It cannot buy a dedicated senior engineer.
- Implied hours if someone divided the support guess by a published rate (the sources do not sell these hours as a retainer): at Lemon’s Colombia senior median of **$34/hour**, $300 is about **8.8 hours**, $500 about **14.7 hours**, $800 about **23.5 hours**. At Lemon’s strong-senior **$43/hour**, the same fees are about **7.0, 11.6, and 18.6 hours**. At the top of Arc’s LatAm average (**$75/hour**), they are about **4.0, 6.7, and 10.7 hours**. At the bottom of the Clutch Colombia agency band that named firms actually use (**$50/hour**), they are **6, 10, and 16 hours**; at **$99/hour**, about **3.0, 5.1, and 8.1 hours**. Applying Ryz Labs’ 160-hour month to Revelo’s senior band implies roughly **$45–$67/hour** all-in ($7,200/160 to $10,700/160). Revelo never states that hourly rate.

### Gaps
- No published 2025–2026 hourly card for Venezuela, and none that isolates “support of an existing logistics product” versus new development.
- Arc does not publish junior/mid/senior rates for Latin America. The global seniority table should not be read as a Caracas or Bogotá price.
- Clutch bands are self-reported agency averages with large minimum project sizes. They are not evidence of a monthly support SKU.
- Terminal’s contractor page, as retrieved, has no “as of” date. The $4,000–$12,000 sentence is marketing, not the median table.

## What do $300, $500, and $800 monthly support retainers buy in hours and response time?

### Takeaway
No retrieved price card has a $300 tier. Published packages at $500 buy **2.5, 8, or 10 hours**, with response from “within a few hours in the vendor’s business day” to **next business day** for critical issues and **up to 5 business days** for normal issues. The one published $800 tier buys **10 hours** and promises that someone can be reached at any hour, while the written response target is **within 2 working days**. Faster-than-a-business-day coverage in the retrieved cards starts around **$2,500–$4,000**, except one Venezuelan Odoo page that claims faster response inside $560 (see the Venezuela section; that page contradicts itself).

### Cited Findings
- Whitten Technology Group lists five engineering retainers. **Care Lite is $500/month for 2.5 hours**, critical response **next business day**, normal response **5 business days**. **Care is $1,000/month for 5 hours**, critical **next business day**, normal **3 business days**. **Grow is $2,500/month for 12 hours**, critical **same business day**, normal **2 business days**. Higher tiers (Fractional Engineer **$4,000**, Embedded Lead **$7,500**) also say same business day for critical work; the retrieved page did not show included hours for those two. The page says the plans are for software the firm built or looks after. It does not state a country. — [WTG service levels](https://whitten-technology.com/service-levels)
- A solo offshore retainer (the author states AU business hours, overlapping UK morning and US East evening) sells **$500/month for 10 hours** of mixed development, L3 support, and assistant work. Unused hours roll up to **50%**. Overflow stays at **$50/hour**. The page says most things are handled within a few hours during those business hours, and that clients get same-day responses on weekdays and Saturday morning. It is not a Latin American agency and not 24/7. — [Lead Steer retainer](https://heyitsmoo.com/services/offshore-tech-retainer)
- SolarDevs publishes SRE retainers: **Essential $500/month for 8 hours**, response **next business day**, on-call scope **business hours**, postmortems quarterly. **Growth $1,500/month for 15 hours**, response **8 business hours**. **Scale $4,000/month for 40 hours**, response **2 hours**, with incident response and an executive report. Unused hours do not roll over. Extra hours are **$125/hour**. The page does not state the team’s country. — [SolarDevs SRE packages](https://www.solardevs.com/solutions/sre-as-a-service/)
- Jigsaw Digital’s terms publish three subscriptions: **10 hours/month for $800**, **40 hours for $3,200**, **80 hours for $6,400** (a flat **$80/hour**). Hours roll over up to **10 hours**. Support is “available 24/7” by WhatsApp and email, and the response commitment is “within a maximum of **2 working days**,” including emergency updates while a developer is on leave (10 days’ notice). An emergency is defined as something that stops a core feature. This is availability of the channel, not a 2-hour fix. — [Jigsaw Digital terms](https://jigsaw.digital/terms)
- Optovo describes ongoing-care retainers as including dependency and security updates, monitoring, tested backups, bug-fix SLAs, “monthly small-feature work (typically **4–16 hours** per tier),” a quarterly review, and a Slack channel with the engineer. It says basic care “starts around **$500/month**,” standard retainers “**$1,500–$3,000/month**,” and 24/7 on-call “scales from there.” It does not publish a $300 or $800 SKU, and it does not pin hours to a dollar tier. The words “around” and “typically” are the vendor’s. — [Optovo ongoing care](https://optovo.co/services/ongoing-care)
- None of the pages above is a Latin American firm. The only retrieved Latin American support menu with hours inside this dollar range is IoniaDev in Venezuela, documented in the Venezuela section: **$95 for 2 consulting hours** (email, 48 hours), **$290 for 8 hours** (priority, 24 hours on the plan card), **$560 for 20 hours**. That is Odoo support, not a custom parcel codebase. — [IoniaDev soporte](https://ioniadev.com/servicios/soporte)

### Inferences
- Dividing published package price by included hours, which the vendors do not always state as an hourly rate: WTG Care Lite is **$200/hour** ($500 / 2.5). The AU solo retainer is **$50/hour** ($500 / 10), and the page also states overflow at $50/hour, so that one is explicit. SolarDevs Essential is **$62.50/hour** ($500 / 8), with a stated overage of $125/hour that is higher than the pack rate. Jigsaw is **$80/hour** ($800 / 10), consistent across its three tiers.
- Mapped onto the founder’s three numbers, using only packages that print both a price and hours: **$300 has no match.** The nearest published dollar amount with hours is IoniaDev at $290 for 8 consulting hours plus backups and monitoring, with a 24-hour priority target, for Odoo. **$500** buys 2.5 hours at next-business-day critical response (WTG), or 8 hours at next-business-day (SolarDevs), or 10 hours with same-day handling inside one person’s Australian weekday (Lead Steer). **$800** buys 10 hours at Jigsaw, with a written reply inside 2 working days even though the channel is open 24/7.
- Those packages are mostly engineering maintenance (fixes and small changes). The founder’s described work is narrower: explain the existing product, handle incidents, change configuration, and train one tenant on a model he already runs. A development retainer’s hours overstate what “how do I use this” support requires, and they understate response-time cost if the tenant expects nights and weekends. Nothing retrieved sells 24/7 senior engineering for a custom logistics app at $800. The first retrieved card with a **2-hour** response is SolarDevs at **$4,000/month**. WTG’s same-business-day critical tier starts at **$2,500/month**.

### Gaps
- No published retainer at exactly $300 was found.
- No Latin American software agency price card was found that says “$500 buys N hours and this SLA” for a custom product. Clutch profiles show hourly bands and minimum project sizes, not retainers.
- “Response time” on these pages is time-to-first-answer or time-to-start, not time-to-repair. None of them publishes an uptime credit for a parcel operation.
- Geography of WTG and SolarDevs was not stated on the retrieved pages. They are evidence of what small retainers contain, not of Bogotá or Valencia prices.

## What do SaaS companies charge for premium support, as a percent of license or as a named tier?

### Takeaway
Where a vendor still prices support as a percent of the subscription, the retrieved official figures are **30% of net license fees** (Salesforce Premier, on India and Sweden pages) and **25% or 35% of annual subscription** (Boomi Premier and Premier Plus, on a UK government listing). Those percents buy faster help-desk response and success resources, not a block of implementation hours. A logistics vendor (Shippit) names a Premium Support add-on and describes 24/7 priority access, but the retrieved text has no dollar amount and no percent.

### Cited Findings
- Salesforce’s India success-plans page lists **Premier Success Plan at 30% of net licence fees**, including expert guidance, health checks, and **24/7 support for business-stopping issues**, and says Premier is bundled with Unlimited Edition. Standard is included in all licences. — [Salesforce Success Plans, India](https://www.salesforce.com/in/services/success-plans/)
- Salesforce’s Sweden pricing page also lists Premier at **30% of net licence fees**. The comparison table on that page says Standard technical support is a response **within two days** on all requests, Premier is a **1-hour** response for business-critical problems, and Signature is **15 minutes** for business-critical problems. Signature is “contact your account executive,” with no percent. Premier is described as included in Unlimited and Agentforce 1 editions. — [Salesforce Success Plans, Sweden](https://www.salesforce.com/se/services/pricing/)
- Salesforce’s US pricing page, retrieved the same day, does **not** print 30%. It says Premier is **included with Core, Advanced, and Max licenses**, still with 24/7 support for business-stopping issues, and Signature is contact-your-account-executive. The page says pricing is subject to change. — [Salesforce Success Plans, United States](https://www.salesforce.com/services/pricing/)
- A Salesforce partner-education page states older packaging: Premier Success **20% of net spend** on Professional and Enterprise, Premier+ **30%**, Premier+ included with Unlimited and Performance. A current Salesforce legal PDF says “Premier+ is a legacy success plan, no longer offered for sale,” and that Premier and Signature are priced per instance and must cover all subscriptions in that instance. The PDF’s response-time table did not extract into a reliable grid; it does say the figures are **target initial response times**, and the flattened text includes a **15-minute** and a **1-hour** target marked 24×7 on the Signature side. Do not treat the partner page’s 20% as the current US list price. — [Salesforce partner note on Premier](https://partners.salesforce.com/s/education/general/Premier_Success); [Salesforce Success Plan product terms (PDF)](https://www.salesforce.com/en-us/wp-content/uploads/sites/4/documents/legal/Agreements/product-specific-terms/salesforce-standard-premier-signature-success.pdf)
- On the UK Digital Marketplace, the supplier listing for Boomi says Standard support is included. **Premier support is 25% of the total annual subscription price** (connections, add-ons, and upgrades included). **Premier Plus is 35%.** Support cannot be bought without the software. Standard: severity 1 in **1 hour, 24/7**, severities 2–4 in **2 business days**, and a cap of **15 cases per year**. Premier: unlimited cases, severity 1 still **1 hour, 24/7**, severities 2–4 in **8 business hours**, coverage **24/5** (Sunday 5pm to Friday 8pm). Premier Plus: severities 2–4 in **4 business hours**, coverage **24/7**. — [Boomi listing on Digital Marketplace](https://www.applytosupply.digitalmarketplace.service.gov.uk/g-cloud/services/872260475179638)
- Shippit’s pricing page describes a **Premium Support** add-on: “24/7 priority access with fastest SLA targets,” a dedicated technical account manager, monthly health checks, adoption planning, and priority incident resolution. The retrieved text says it is available as an add-on or on selected high-volume Pro plans. No dollar figure and no percent were in that text. Shippit is shipping software, so this is the closest logistics-vendor example found, and it is incomplete on price. — [Shippit pricing](https://www.shippit.com/pricing)
- A pricing-advisory article dated **10 October 2025** says traditional software maintenance was “usually **15–25%** of the initial license cost,” and that SaaS often folds support into the subscription. It cites Gainsight’s **2023** Customer Success Industry Report for two claims: **72%** of SaaS companies include some customer-success service in the base subscription, and **64%** offer premium support tiers at extra cost. Those are the article’s citations of a 2023 report, not a 2025–2026 vendor price. — [Monetizely, 10 Oct 2025](https://www.getmonetizely.com/articles/how-should-you-structure-subscription-pricing-for-customer-success-and-support-services); the 15–25% band is repeated in [Monetizely’s maintenance-revenue article](https://www.getmonetizely.com/articles/how-to-calculate-maintenance-and-support-revenue-a-guide-for-saas-executives)

### Inferences
- Percent-of-license support and an hour-block retainer are different products. Salesforce and Boomi sell a faster queue, phone or chat, and some success management. They do not sell “8 hours of a senior engineer.” If a license were small, 25–30% of it would be a small dollar amount; if the license were a full Salesforce org, 30% would dwarf $800. This research did not take a Deliservy license fee as an input, so the percent cannot be turned into a dollar support fee here.
- The founder’s $300–$800 guess lines up in dollars with small engineering retainers (a handful of hours, business-day response), not with enterprise premier support. Enterprise premier support is either a large percent of a large contract or, on the flat-fee cards, a multi-thousand-dollar tier once the promise is same-day or 2-hour response.
- Shippit shows that a parcel/shipping SaaS will name 24/7 priority support as a paid tier. It does not show what that tier costs.

### Gaps
- No public price, in dollars or percent, was retrieved for premium support from a last-mile or parcel SaaS (no usable figure for Onfleet, Circuit, Tookan, or similar). Shippit’s tier has no number in the text retrieved.
- Salesforce’s own pages disagree by country: 30% of net license in India and Sweden, versus “included with Core, Advanced, and Max” in the United States with no percent. The partner page’s 20% looks legacy next to the PDF that retires Premier+.
- Gainsight figures are from 2023, via a secondary article. They are not a 2026 rate card.

## What is a fair split between platform costs and human support, and is $200 a cost or a price?

### Takeaway
Store fees and push messaging are small. A modest virtual server is tens of dollars. Google maps and routes are free up to published monthly caps and then metered. SMS into Venezuela, at Twilio’s October 2026 rate of **$0.2257 per segment**, is the line that can consume an entire $200 by itself at under 1,000 messages. $200 can therefore be a real vendor-cost envelope for a light month. It is not a market price for a person, and it is not a safe cap if the tenant’s customers are texted.

### Cited Findings
- Apple Developer Program is **$99 USD per year**. The Enterprise Program is **$299 USD per year**. Apple says prices may vary by region and are shown in local currency at enrollment. $99/year is **$8.25 per month** if spread evenly; Apple bills it annually. — [Apple program enrollment](https://developer.apple.com/help/account/membership/program-enrollment/)
- Google Play Console charges a **US$25 one-time registration fee**. The official help page retrieved does not describe an ongoing monthly store fee. — [Play Console registration](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6112435)
- Firebase’s pricing page lists **Cloud Messaging (FCM) as no-cost** on both the Spark and Blaze plans. Phone authentication is **billed per SMS sent** on Blaze and is not available as a no-cost Spark line in the retrieved table (“Not applicable” on Spark). Other authentication is no-cost, and Identity Platform monthly active users are no-cost up to **50,000**, then Google Cloud pricing. Firestore’s no-cost caps include **1 GiB** stored, **10 GiB/month** egress, **20,000 writes/day**, **50,000 reads/day**, **20,000 deletes/day**. Cloud Functions on Blaze are no-cost up to **2 million invocations/month**, then **$0.40 per million**. — [Firebase pricing](https://firebase.google.com/pricing)
- Google Maps Platform core list, pay-as-you-go, prices per 1,000 billable events after a free cap. **Maps SDK is unlimited at no cost.** **Dynamic Maps** (web map loads): **10,000 free**, then **$7.00** per 1,000 up to 100,000. **Geocoding**: **10,000 free**, then **$5.00** per 1,000. **Routes: Compute Routes Essentials** and **Compute Route Matrix Essentials**: **10,000 free**, then **$5.00** per 1,000. **Routes Enterprise**: **1,000 free**, then **$15.00** per 1,000. **Route Optimization – Fleet Routing**: **1,000 free**, then **$30.00** per 1,000 up to 100,000. **Places autocomplete**: **10,000 free**, then **$2.83** per 1,000. Google’s own worked example: 200,000 autocomplete requests in a month cost **$481.70** after the free cap. Volume is aggregated across projects on the billing account. — [Google Maps Platform pricing](https://developers.google.com/maps/billing-and-pricing/pricing)
- Twilio’s United States SMS page, “pricing current as of October 2026,” is **$0.0083 per segment** outbound or inbound on long codes, toll-free, and short codes, **plus carrier fees**. Example carrier add-ons on long-code outbound SMS: AT&T **$0.0035**, T-Mobile **$0.0045**, Verizon **$0.005**. A failed message adds **$0.001**. A US long-code lease is **$1.15/month**. — [Twilio SMS pricing, United States](https://www.twilio.com/en-us/sms/pricing/us)
- Twilio’s Venezuela SMS page, also “pricing current as of October 2026,” is **$0.2257 per outbound segment** for international numbers and for alphanumeric sender IDs. Inbound SMS and MMS cells are blank. A failed message adds **$0.001**. Optional SMS pumping protection is **$0.025** per outbound message. An international-number lease starts at **$1.15/month**. — [Twilio SMS pricing, Venezuela](https://www.twilio.com/en-us/sms/pricing/ve)
- DigitalOcean basic virtual machines, with monthly caps, include **$6/month** (1 GiB, 1 vCPU, 25 GiB SSD, 1,000 GiB transfer), **$12** (2 GiB), **$24** (4 GiB, 2 vCPU, 80 GiB SSD, 4,000 GiB transfer), and **$48** (8 GiB, 4 vCPU). Weekly backups are **20%** of droplet cost; daily backups are **30%**. Snapshots are **$0.06/GB per month**. The page says per-second billing with a monthly cap took effect **1 January 2026**. These are unmanaged machines: the customer runs the operating system. — [DigitalOcean Droplets](https://www.digitalocean.com/pricing/droplets)

### Inferences
- A light vendor stack, using only those list prices, can sit well under $200. Example built only to test the guess, not as a recommended bill: Apple **$8.25** (the annual fee spread over 12 months), Play **$0** after the one-time $25, one 4 GiB droplet **$24**, weekly backups at 20% = **$4.80**, FCM **$0**, Maps SDK **$0**, and geocoding plus basic routes **$0** if each stays inside 10,000 events. That subtotal is about **$37** before SMS. Two hundred Venezuela SMS segments at $0.2257 are **$45.14**. Together that is still under $100. In this light month, $200 is more than the vendor cost, so it would already contain margin if someone charged it as a flat “operating” fee. It still contains no human support.
- The same $200 is used up by SMS alone at about **886 Venezuela segments** (886 × $0.2257 = $199.97). One thousand segments cost **$225.70**, which is over the guess before hosting, stores, or maps. US SMS is a different product: 1,000 segments at $0.0083 are **$8.30** before carrier fees of roughly $3.50–$5.00. A parcel app texting Venezuelan phones cannot use the US rate.
- Maps are cheap only inside the free caps. A mobile map drawn with the Maps SDK does not, by itself, create a Maps bill. Geocoding, route calculation, and especially fleet routing do. Four thousand fleet-routing events after the 1,000 free cap, at $30 per 1,000, are **$120**. Google’s autocomplete example of **$481.70** shows a single SKU crossing $200 without any unusual enterprise contract. Whether $200 covers maps depends on which APIs the app calls and how many times, which this research did not measure in Deliservy’s traffic.
- Split that the evidence supports, without picking Deliservy’s price: store fees plus a small server plus push notifications are a **tens-of-dollars cost**. Human support at published LatAm and small-retainer rates is a **separate hundreds-of-dollars line**, because even 8–10 hours of a senior Colombia contractor or a published $500 pack exceeds the whole infrastructure example above. SMS to Venezuela is a **third line** that can be larger than either, and it is a pass-through cost, not a support fee. Charging one blended “operate the system” number hides which of the three is inside it.

### Gaps
- No Deliservy invoice or usage report was in scope, so the $200 test uses list prices and round volumes, not this product’s actual map calls or message counts.
- Managed database, object storage, error monitoring, a domain, a payment gateway, and WhatsApp Business API (more commonly used than SMS with Venezuelan customers) were not priced from official pages in this pass. Any of those can move the vendor total.
- Firebase phone-auth SMS rates by country were not opened. The pricing page only says phone auth is billed per SMS.
- Google Play’s service fee on in-app purchases was not taken from an official page in this pass. It does not matter for a free app that does not sell through Play Billing. The $25 registration fee is the store cost that was confirmed.
- DigitalOcean is an illustration of public VPS prices, not evidence of where this platform is hosted.

## What do Venezuelan B2B buyers pay monthly in USD for software plus support?

### Takeaway
Evidence is thin and mostly vendor list prices, not surveys of what buyers actually pay. Local cloud POS and ERP subscriptions that include chat or “24/7” support cluster at **$15–$90 per month**. The only retrieved Venezuelan page that sells human support by the hour in this neighborhood is an Odoo partner at **$95, $290, and $560**. Nothing retrieved shows a Valencia company paying a few hundred dollars a month for support of a custom parcel platform.

### Cited Findings
- Neto Business, a Venezuelan POS and inventory product, lists a paid plan at **$15 per month**, billed annually at **$180 per year**, payable in bolívares at the vendor’s displayed “tasa oficial BCV,” in **USD cash**, Zelle, Binance Pay (USDT), or cards. The $15 plan includes “soporte VIP prioritario por WhatsApp,” unlimited cashiers, and the POS features. The page displayed **Bs 887.30 per USD** as that BCV rate; it does not date the rate. Multi-branch pricing is “descuentos especiales por volumen,” with no number, and includes cashier training and a dedicated commercial contact in Venezuela. This is a multi-tenant SaaS fee with chat support, not engineering hours. — [Neto Business plans](https://netobusiness.com/planes)
- PosUp, marketed as cloud ERP for Venezuela, lists **Essential at $30/month** (1 POS, 1 warehouse, 1 branch, up to 2,500 SKUs, unlimited users, “soporte estándar”) and **Professional at $90/month**. An Enterprise heading mentions multi-branch, customizations, and a dedicated account; the retrieved text has **no Enterprise price**. The marketing page says support is **24/7/365**. Feature bullets under the $90 plan were not in the retrieved text, so only the price and the Essential inclusions are used here. — [PosUp Venezuela](https://posupven.com/)
- IoniaDev publishes Odoo support in Venezuela, in USD per month. **Básico $95**: email, **48-hour** response, minor updates, monthly backup, basic monitoring, knowledge base, **2 hours** of consulting. **Profesional $290**: priority support with **24-hour** response on the plan card, phone calls, automatic updates, weekly backup, “monitoreo proactivo 24/7,” quarterly optimization, **8 hours** of consulting, basic new features. **Enterprise $560**: the plan card says **24/7 with immediate response**, a direct emergency line, daily redundant backups, **20 hours** of consulting, custom development, a dedicated consultant, and a **99.9% SLA**. A separate SLA block on the same page says Basic **48 hours**, Professional **24 hours**, and Enterprise **2 hours** (“atención inmediata 24/7”). The header says “soporte 24/7 garantizado” and shows a struck-through **$120** next to **$95**. The page says the author is an official Odoo partner with 10+ years; that claim was not checked against Odoo’s partner directory in this pass. Testimonials name Venezuelan businesses; they are the vendor’s marketing. — [IoniaDev soporte](https://ioniadev.com/servicios/soporte)
- J2 Software POS says it serves Colombia and Venezuela and lists prices only in Colombian pesos: **Emprendedor $49,000 per month** (1 site, 2 users, no priority-support line in the retrieved bullets) and **Pro $79,000 per month**, which adds multi-site, unlimited users, WhatsApp notifications, and **soporte prioritario**. A DIAN digital certificate is an extra **$180,000 COP** for one year. No USD price and no exchange rate were retrieved, so these are not converted. — [J2 Software POS](https://www.j2softwarepos.com/)
- NexusCommerce lists administrative SaaS at **$10, $30, and $40 per month**, and accountant bundles at **$15, $35, and $70**, with “soporte prioritario” named on the **$70** plan. The same page says Mexico and Panama are the current focus and “pronto estaremos en Venezuela.” It is not evidence of a product already being bought in Venezuela. — [Automatiza B2B Venezuela page](https://automatizab2b.com/ve/)

### Inferences
- Against these local list prices, a **$300–$800 support-only** fee is high for Venezuelan packaged software: it is 4 to 10 times PosUp’s $90 Professional plan, and it overlaps IoniaDev’s professional and enterprise Odoo retainers rather than the $15–$90 SaaS plans. IoniaDev at **$290** is the closest published Venezuelan analogue to the bottom of the founder’s support guess, and it includes 8 consulting hours plus caretaking, at a 24-hour response target, for a packaged ERP. IoniaDev at **$560** is inside the guess and is the only local card that claims a dedicated consultant and a 2-hour or “immediate” target. Dividing those prices by the consulting hours alone ($95/2, $290/8, $560/20) gives about **$48, $36, and $28 per hour**, but that division ignores backups, monitoring, and updates bundled in the fee, so the implied rate of the consulting hour is overstated.
- The **$200 operating** guess is also high relative to local SaaS that already includes hosting and chat support at $15–$90. Those products are not a custom parcel platform with Google routes and SMS, so the comparison shows buyer expectations, not the vendor cost stack in the previous section. A Venezuelan buyer looking at Neto or PosUp has been shown software-plus-support well below $200. That does not mean a custom logistics tenant’s maps and SMS cost less than $200.
- USD cash, Zelle, and USDT are normal collection methods on the Neto page. The support question can be discussed in dollars without assuming a bolívar invoice. The BCV figure on that page is the vendor’s displayed rate on the day the page was retrieved, not an official rate verified here.

### Gaps
- No news article, job post, or buyer survey was found on what Venezuelan companies paid in USD cash for software support in 2025–2026. The record is vendor price pages.
- No Venezuelan or Colombian page was found for support of a custom parcel or last-mile product. Odoo and POS are the nearest local categories, and they are packaged software.
- IoniaDev’s plan card and its SLA grid do not match on Enterprise response (immediate versus 2 hours), and the 24/7 headline does not match the Basic plan’s 48-hour email SLA. Treat the hours and the dollar amounts as the usable facts, and the uptime and “immediate” wording as unverified marketing.
- PosUp’s $90 plan contents and Enterprise price were not fully retrieved. J2’s peso prices were not converted. NexusCommerce should not be counted as an in-market Venezuela price.
